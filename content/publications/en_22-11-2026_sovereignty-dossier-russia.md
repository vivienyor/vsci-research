## 2. Vector-by-Vector Structural Audit

### 🛡️ Vector C — Cyber & Cognitive Capability (`0.90` | Contribution: `0.2250`)
The strongest contour of Russian agency. National Computer Emergency Response Teams (CERT/SOC) operate in a state of continuous combat readiness, repelling sustained distributed attacks against Critical Information Infrastructure (CII). High protection of the semantic perimeter is secured through the dominance of domestic social networks and search engines, minimizing external algorithmic rent extraction.

### 🏭 Vector I — Physical & Energetic Infrastructure (`0.75` | Contribution: `0.2250`)
High architectural autonomy of the national internet segment (Runet) has been achieved through deep packet inspection infrastructure (TSPU), a sovereign Domain Name System (NSDI), and surplus baseload power generation (nuclear and hydro) supporting commercial and state data centers. The primary vulnerability remains reliance on imported components for telecommunications and server hardware.

### 💻 Vector S — Technological & Logical Stack (`0.65` | Contribution: `0.1950`)
Forced technological decoupling triggered a rapid migration of the public sector, large enterprises, and CII entities onto domestic Linux distributions (Astra Linux, Alt, RedOS) and sovereign DBMS platforms. Russia belongs to a narrow club of nations possessing full-fledged domestic search, cloud, and AI ecosystems (Yandex, Sber). The core growth constraint is the lack of domestic advanced photolithography and reliance on foreign processor architectures.

### 📉 Vector V — Volatility & Shock Resilience (`0.60` | Contribution: `0.0600`)
An elevated baseline volatility score reflects intense external sanctions and disconnection from Western financial clearing rails. However, at the operational level, this shock has been effectively dampened through sovereign financial infrastructure (NSPK, Mir payment system, SPFS), resource self-sufficiency, and the transition of cross-border trade to national currencies (primarily the Chinese yuan).
